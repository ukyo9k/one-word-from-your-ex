## The entry

One word. The word their ex would use to describe them:

> **almost**

## Why "almost"

Nobody's ex describes them with a paragraph. They say: *"he was... almost."*

- **It lands instantly.** Six letters, no setup, no explanation — and no near synonym keeps the edge. "Nearly" is a shrug; "almost" is a verdict.
- **It stings because it is true.** Close enough to matter, not enough to keep. The almost-the-one, the almost-worked-out, the almost-forever.
- **It is relatable.** Everyone has been an "almost" — or had one. It names something people have lived through, which is exactly what makes a judge laugh and wince at the same time.
- **It is honest, not mean.** The entrant plainly knows it, and that self-knowledge is the whole joke.

## How it works

`main.py` writes the single word to `/output/entry.txt` — deterministically, with no input, no clock, no randomness and no network. Run it twice and the output is byte-identical.

```sh
python3 main.py
cat /output/entry.txt   # almost
```
